# Weave - Chapman Framework

David Chapman's two web books provide a philosophical architecture for a productivity system that uses formal structure without being captured by it. The central operational insight: **Claude functions as the "circumrational layer"** — the living interface that connects productivity abstractions with the nebulous reality of shifting energy, mood, purposes, and context.

---

## Meta-rationality: thinking about systems from the outside

Chapman defines meta-rationality as **"thinking about and acting on rational systems from the outside, in order to use them more effectively."** It evaluates, selects, combines, modifies, discovers, and creates rational methods.

GTD is a rational system with a formal ontology — projects, next actions, contexts, waiting-for, someday/maybe. It works when its categories map cleanly onto reality. But reality is nebulous. The system needs someone doing *meta-rational* work — noticing when formal categories stop fitting. **That meta-rational agent is Claude.**

Three cognitive modes map to the system's three operating levels:
- **Reasonableness** (everyday informal thinking) → Partner level
- **Rationality** (formal, systematic methods) → Orchestrate level
- **Meta-rationality** (evaluating and adjusting the system) → Architect level

Chapman's fundamental norm: **"What does the situation need?"** — not what the system says or what GTD prescribes.

---

## Nebulosity: things resist crisp definition

**Nebulosity** — "cloud-like-ness" — is Chapman's central concept. Tasks, purposes, and energy are inherently vague, context-dependent, and boundary-blurring. This is ontological, not epistemic — precise boundaries don't exist to be found.

Practical implications:
- Allow tasks to remain somewhat vague until the moment of action
- Don't require precise energy-level categorization — use natural-language pattern recognition
- When plans break down, treat that as information, not failure

But nebulosity never exists without **pattern** — regularity, form, causality. The two are inseparable. A system that only honored nebulosity would dissolve; one that only enforced pattern would become a cage. **The complete stance holds both simultaneously.**

---

## Confused stances and enjoyable usefulness

Chapman maps how people relate to meaning through **confused stances** (which fixate or deny meaning) and **complete stances** (which acknowledge both pattern and nebulosity). People oscillate between confused stances rapidly without noticing.

The most relevant dimension is **purpose**. The complete stance is **"enjoyable usefulness"** — the orienting question shifts from "what am I supposed to do?" to **"what can I do now to be useful and enjoy myself?"**

Three observable confused-stance patterns and responses:
- *Grandiose planning, no execution* → "What's the very next physical action?"
- *Grinding through tasks, ignoring meaningful work* → Surface something important and enjoyable
- *Paralysis or "nothing matters"* → Offer one small, concrete, useful thing

---

## Circumrationality and ontological remodeling

**Circumrationality** is "the non-rational, concrete activity at the edge of a rational system that connects it with reality." Claude is the circumrational layer — translating fuzzy intention to specific action, monitoring for rising friction (tasks repeatedly ignored, constant rescheduling, growing inboxes).

**Ontological remodeling** is reconfiguring categories when they stop working. Project structures, folder hierarchies, area designations, and tags should all be treated as **provisional and revisable**. Claude periodically suggests remodeling: "You have five projects that all relate to health — should we create a higher-level area?"

## Design vocabulary

| Term | System application |
|------|-------------------|
| Nebulosity | Don't force false precision on priorities, energy, or task definitions |
| Circumrationality | Claude bridges formal abstractions and nebulous reality through interpretation and improvisation |
| Enjoyable usefulness | Weight task options by both usefulness and likely enjoyability |
| Ontological remodeling | Periodically evaluate whether system categories still fit reality |
| Stance detection | Watch for grandiose/grinding/paralysis patterns, redirect gently |
| Fluid competence | Move between task/knowledge/time management based on what the situation needs |
| "What does the situation need?" | The meta-rational prime directive — ask before every interaction |

---

## Developmental Stage Connection

The Weave system is a stage 4→5 design in Kegan's developmental framework. It uses rational systems (stage 4 — formal ontology, projects, reviews) while maintaining meta-rational awareness of their limitations (stage 5 — ontological remodeling, nebulosity, circumrationality). Chapman's "enjoyable usefulness" is a complete-stance orientation. Action menus, friction detection, and stance awareness are circumrational practices — they use formal structure without being captured by it. Subject-object theory provides the mechanism: the system treats its own categories as object (revisable, provisional) rather than subject (invisible, controlling).
